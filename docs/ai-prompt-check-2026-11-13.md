# Morning Lamp: AI prompt list and first check (Nov 13, 2026)

What people are likely to ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode in our space, and who those tools name today.

**Result: Morning Lamp was named in 0 of 26 answers.** That is expected for a site a few weeks old. The useful part is who does get named, and where the tools get those names from.

## How this was done

- The prompts come from real Google data (search volumes and autocomplete questions), rewritten the way people type into a chat tool. Nobody outside OpenAI can see actual ChatGPT prompts, so this list is a best estimate.
- Each prompt was run once, on Nov 13, 2026. AI answers change from run to run, so treat one run as a snapshot.
- ChatGPT was run in a temporary chat with personalization switched off, so Rob's own chat history could not tilt it. Perplexity and Google AI Mode were run signed in.
- 26 answers were read in full: 12 on ChatGPT, 7 on Perplexity, 7 on Google AI Mode. The other prompts on the list were not run (Chrome stalled under the load); they are marked below.

## Three openings where nobody owns the answer yet

1. **"A daily Bible study that explains the history and context behind each verse" (prompt 8).** The tools named a podcast (The Bible Recap), audio guides (Through the Word, Thru the Bible), commentaries (Enduring Word) and a study Bible site (The Context Bible). None named a daily email. This is exactly what Morning Lamp is.
2. **"Not fluffy, with real Bible depth and church history" (prompt 20).** Google and Perplexity named only books (Be Thou My Vision, For the Love of God, Voices from the Past, The Valley of Vision). No daily email was named.
3. **"Personalized to what I'm going through" (prompt 16).** Google named AI apps (Shep, FaithGPT, The Scripture Nook). ChatGPT did not recommend anything: it offered to build the study itself. Last Night's Lamp fits this prompt, but ChatGPT is a direct competitor on it.

The generic prompts ("best daily devotional email") are owned by ministries with decades of mentions. Those are the hardest to break into.

## Where the tools get their names

The sites cited beside the answers, most often first:

- The product's own site (ourdailybread.org, navigators.org, todayintheword.org, freedailybiblestudy.com).
- **Reddit.** Cited by Google on prompts 1, 2, 4 and 16, and by Perplexity on 16 and 20.
- **List articles and roundups:** crosswalk.com, kevinhalloran.net, thegospelcoalition.org, learn.ligonier.org, reformedreader.wordpress.com, themondaychristian.com.
- App stores and YouTube, for app prompts.
